The big picture

More than moving
things from A to B.

Logistics and supply chain management are closely related, but they are not interchangeable. Understanding the distinction is the first step to understanding how modern business works.

The operation

Logistics

Logistics focuses on planning, implementing and controlling the efficient flow and storage of goods, services and related information from origin to consumption.

In practice: transportation, warehousing, inventory handling, order fulfilment and delivery.

The whole system

Supply Chain

Supply chain management coordinates the network of organizations, activities, resources and information involved in creating and delivering a product or service.

In practice: sourcing, procurement, production, logistics, distribution and customer relationships.

Pressure points

Where the system
gets tested.

Resilient supply chains must balance cost, speed, reliability and sustainability — often under conditions they cannot fully control.

01

Disruption & uncertainty

Geopolitical tensions, extreme weather, port congestion and supplier failures can interrupt the flow of goods.

02

Cost & visibility

Fuel, labour, storage and inventory costs pressure margins. Limited end-to-end visibility makes decisions harder.

03

Technology & talent

Digital transformation requires interoperable systems, reliable data, cybersecurity and a skilled workforce.

The next chapter

Building a smarter,
stronger flow.

The future of logistics is not simply faster delivery. It is a more visible, adaptive and responsible supply network.

Strategic directions
  • AI & predictive analytics
    Forecast demand, optimize routes and anticipate disruption.
  • End-to-end visibility
    Connect data across suppliers, carriers, warehouses and customers.
  • Resilience by design
    Diversify suppliers, plan scenarios and build flexible capacity.
  • Green logistics
    Improve load factors, energy efficiency, packaging and reverse flows.
